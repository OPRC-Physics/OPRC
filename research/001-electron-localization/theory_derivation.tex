\section{Theoretical Framework: The Kronig-Penney Model}
To demonstrate the absence of free electrons in an ideal insulator, we solve the time-independent Schrödinger equation:
$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2\psi}{dx^2} + V(x)\psi = E\psi$$
where $V(x)$ is a periodic square-well potential representing the atomic lattice. 

\subsection{Energy Gap Formation}
The solution leads to the transcendental equation:
$$P \frac{\sin(\alpha a)}{\alpha a} + \cos(\alpha a) = \cos(ka)$$
where $P$ is the "scattering power" of the potential barrier. In an ideal insulator, the value of $P$ is sufficiently high, and the energy $E$ falls within the "forbidden" region where no real solution for the wave vector $k$ exists. Consequently, electrons are trapped in the valence states with zero probability of occupying the conduction band at $0K$.
\section{Phase 2: Statistical Occupancy and Thermal Excitation}
The concentration of electrons in the conduction band $n_c$ for an insulator is governed by the Fermi-Dirac distribution:
$$f(E) = \frac{1}{e^{(E-E_F)/k_B T} + 1}$$

\subsection{Carrier Concentration Proof}
For an ideal insulator where the band gap $E_g \gg k_B T$, the number of electrons excited to the conduction band is:
$$n_c \approx N_c e^{-(E_g / 2k_B T)}$$
where $N_c$ is the effective density of states. 

\paragraph{Calculation:}
Taking a standard insulator gap $E_g = 5\text{ eV}$ and room temperature $T = 300\text{K}$ ($k_B T \approx 0.025\text{ eV}$):
$$\frac{E_g}{2k_B T} = \frac{5}{0.05} = 200$$
$$n_c \propto e^{-200} \approx 10^{-87}$$

This mathematically infinitesimal value confirms that, for all physical intents and purposes, the conduction band is empty and no free electrons exist.
\section{Phase 3: Real-Space Localization via Wannier Functions}
To demonstrate that electrons in an insulator are "stuck" to atomic cores, we transform the delocalized Bloch states $\psi_{n\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{r})$ into localized Wannier functions $w_n(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{R})$:
$$w_n(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{R}) = \frac{V}{(2\pi)^3} \int_{BZ} e^{-i\mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{R}} \psi_{n\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{r}) d\mathbf{k}$$

\subsection{The Localization Proof}
In a metal, these functions are broad and overlap significantly. In an ideal insulator, due to the lack of conduction band occupancy (Phase 2), the electronic density is composed entirely of these Wannier states centered at lattice sites $\mathbf{R}$. The decay of these functions is exponential:
$$|w_n(\mathbf{r})| \sim e^{-h|\mathbf{r}|}$$
where $h$ is related to the magnitude of the band gap $E_g$. This exponential decay mathematically defines the absence of "free" electrons, as the probability density vanishes rapidly away from the atomic center.
